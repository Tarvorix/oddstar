### 5.1 A dusty background galaxy at the 2010 position

**The finding** [cand] §3.5.1. W3 and W4 were measured only in 2010. In 2010 the star was at (43.548213°, +5.882865°) for J2010.33, the same position I computed above.

A stationary, obscured, dusty galaxy or type-2 AGN at that position would now be 1.6″ from the star, by [cand]'s figure "in 2026". My own computation gives 1.73″ at 2026.88, along the motion vector. The Legacy Survey DR10 Tractor fit forces a PSF at the J2016.0 position, so it would not separate the two.

[cand] also gives the photosphere-subtracted excess colours as (W1−W2) ≈ +1.81 and (W2−W3) ≈ +2.72. These lie in the AGN/ULIRG locus. The v170 run gives +1.41 and +2.26 for the same quantities (`extragalactic_colours` above). The two sets differ, and both are in that region.

**What has been done:**

- `centroid_offset` measured the W3 and W4 image centroids against the propagated Gaia position. The offsets are W3 0.17″ ± 0.04″ and W4 0.80″ ± 0.48″. [adv] got 0.34″ (W3) and 0.44″ (W4) at J2010.3346, with a W4 blank-patch error of about 0.6″ per axis. These measure where the 2010 light is. A source 0–1″ from the 2010 star position is not excluded.
- The NEOWISE track test (`neighbour_signal`, NEOWISE part) shows that the **2014–2024 W1/W2 variable flux moves with the star**: measured pull 0.009 ± 0.017″, against 0.346″ expected for a stationary source carrying it. The stationary flux share is 0.031 ± 0.009. This tests W1/W2 only. It says nothing about the 2010 W3/W4 flux.
- The `radio_counterpart` check passed, with no NVSS source within 10″. Its own note says fewer than 10% of dusty galaxies are radio-loud.
- `chance_alignment` gives P = 0.0002 for a W4 source within 4.2″, from the AllWISE density.
- PS1 and the Legacy Survey found no extended neighbour and no faint point source at 1.8–8″ (`deep_imaging`). This is a seeing-limited screen.

**What has not been done:**

- No sub-arcsecond image of the field exists in MAST (`deep_imaging` query ok, 0 observations).
- No 12–22 µm measurement after 2010 exists (`second_instrument`).
- Neither the optical plate images (the new plate-image module) nor a PSF fit at the fixed 2010 position in the Legacy Survey images have been applied to this star.
- SPHEREx (§6.4) cannot help here. Its pixels are 6.15″, so it measures the blended light within one pixel.

### 5.2 Age from the motion

**What the motion says.** The star lags the Sun by 106 km/s in V and moves at 103 km/s against the local standard of rest. That is thick-disc or halo kinematics. Stars that move like this are old, typically several Gyr.

**Consequence.** A young star with a primordial or debris disc does not fit. This is a kinematic argument, not a measured age. Under config 1.8.0 the `youth` check passes on exactly this argument: tangential velocity 114.6 km/s against an old-population limit of 85 km/s. It was `not_evaluated` under 1.7.0. ### 5.3 Over-luminosity for a single K4V dwarf

**The finding** [cand] §3.5.1:

- M_G = 6.532, with L = 0.182–0.223 L☉ (pipeline and Gaia FLAME). This is 0.30–0.45 mag brighter than a single solar-metallicity K4V at 4510 K (0.15–0.16 L☉, M_G ≈ 6.85–7.00).
- BP−RP = 1.354 does not agree with G−Ks = 2.459, which points to about 4050 K.
- Both fit an unresolved K4V + M binary. Such a binary would inflate the Ks-anchored photosphere and so change the subtracted excess.

**Other values on file, which disagree with the finding and with each other:**

- [dossier] `extinction_cases` compares the star with the pipeline's own dwarf sequence at its dereddened colour, not at Teff.
  - With the applied E(B−V) = 0.030: (BP−RP)₀ = 1.316 (K4V, about 4643 K). The star is **0.06 mag fainter** than the sequence (6.53 against 6.47).
  - With the full line-of-sight E(B−V) = 0.208: K2V. The star is 0.29 mag fainter.
- The `underluminous` channel predicts M_G = 5.37 from XP and finds the star 1.16 mag fainter, at σ = 1.47, so not flagged.
- LAMOST classes the spectrum K5 at S/N 7.6.

So the star's luminosity relative to a single dwarf depends on whether the reference is Teff (over-luminous by 0.30–0.45 mag) or colour (about 0.06 mag faint). The answer also depends on the reddening, between E(B−V) 0.030 and 0.208, because no 3D dust map constrains it.

**What has been done:**

- `binary` was made `not_evaluated` instead of passing (lesson L003), because the faint-star RVS scatter of 6.13 km/s has no p-value.
- RUWE is 1.078 and nss_flag is 0. These exclude neither a close companion nor a low-luminosity one.

**What has not been done:**

- No multi-epoch RVs.
- No SED fit with a two-component (K + M) photosphere to see how much of the W1/W2 excess survives.
- No high-resolution spectrum for Teff, log g, Li or activity.
- No 3D-dust distance-resolved reddening.

### 5.4 Leading natural explanation, neither confirmed nor excluded: an old close binary with warm dust

**Why it fits.** The facts above fit together in one known picture:

- an old K dwarf (§5.2);
- a photometric period near 7 d (`known_variable`: 7.035 d; §6.1: about 6.8 d in 2021);
- 0.30–0.45 mag over-luminosity against a single K4V at its Teff (§5.3).

This is what a close, tidally locked binary looks like. Tidal locking would also explain fast rotation in an old star.

**Known precedent.** A strong, variable warm-dust excess around an old close binary is a known, rare class. The standard example is BD+20 307.

**What it is.** This is the leading natural explanation on file. It is not confirmed and not excluded.

**What argues against it.** §6.1 finds the TESS variability incoherent from sector to sector and without eclipses. That is more like evolving spots than an orbital signal, though spots on a tidally locked star would still need the companion.

**What would decide it:**

1. Radial velocities at two or three epochs a few days apart. A close orbit of a few days gives tens of km/s.
2. An eclipse, or a stable ellipsoidal signal at twice the photometric frequency, in further TESS or ground photometry. §6.1 finds neither in the 7 sectors.

## 7. What this does not show

- **No physical link between the two signals.** It does not show that the 2010 W3/W4 excess and the 2017–2022 W1/W2 brightening are the same thing. They were measured at different wavelengths, years apart, and no W3/W4 measurement exists after 2010.
- **No rare object.** It does not show that the star is unusual among stars in general. The W1/W2 variability is already in VarWISE and the optical variability in VSX (ROT). The outlier score reuses the same WISE numbers. [adv] finding 5 says the repeated 1e-12 false-alarm values need their calibration range, effective trials and injection population stated before they carry weight.
- **The star itself is not characterised.** It does not show the star's age or companions.
  - The kinematics argue for an old star (§5.2), but no age is measured.
  - The binary check is open.
  - The close-binary explanation of §5.4 is neither confirmed nor excluded.
  - The luminosity comparison depends on the reference and the reddening (§5.3).
  - There is one low-S/N spectrum.
- **No confirmation of the 22 µm excess by any second instrument.** SPHEREx stops at 5 µm and blends everything within 6″. Its verdict (§6.4, §6.5) is a change at 3 to 5 µm since 2010, which the rule fixed beforehand does not count as a confirmation.
- **No optical event is established.** The 2017–2022 optical changes are 0.05–0.09 mag and anti-correlated with W2. The sector-4 TESS bin is below threshold and sits at a segment edge.
- **A Tier C is not an anomaly finding.** Tier C means "not explained by the checks that could be run, with holes open". It is not evidence for any particular cause.
