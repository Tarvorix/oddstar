## 5628404318169472 / AllWISE J025411.56+055258.2

### Reproduced measurements

- **Identity.** Gaia gives parallax 4.314876 ± 0.016152 mas, proper motion (+37.914, −97.175) mas yr⁻¹, and tangential speed 114.60 km s⁻¹. The AllWISE position is 0.059″ from the position propagated to the catalog’s J2010.57 mean epoch, consistent with a stellar counterpart. Gaia GSP-Phot gives 4510 K and [M/H] = +0.036. The uncorrected absolute G magnitude is 6.605 and the Gaia extinction-corrected value is 6.530. These support a K-dwarf interpretation. The data in hand leave the spectral subtype, age and dynamical population uncertain. [Gaia DR3 stellar-parameter documentation](https://gea.esac.esa.int/archive/documentation/GDR3/Data_analysis/chap_cu8par/sec_cu8par_apsis/ssec_cu8par_apsis_gspphot.html)
- **Cryogenic excess.** AllWISE reports W3 = 8.701 ± 0.026 and W4 = 7.290 ± 0.102 mag. A 4510 K photosphere normalized at the independently retrieved 2MASS Ks = 10.971 predicts 1.89 and 0.55 mJy, versus 10.48 ± 0.25 and 10.15 ± 0.95 mJy observed, respectively. The ratios are **5.54× and 18.31×**. Varying the photospheric temperature from 3800 to 5500 K gives 4.8–6.4× and 15.6–21.2×. These flux errors are statistical. Broad-band response and color corrections change the physical fit, with uncertainties of order 10% in the W3/W4 magnitude-to-flux conversions. [WISE photometric calibration](https://irsa.ipac.caltech.edu/data/WISE/docs/release/All-Sky/expsup/sec4_4h.html)
- **Exposure-level repeatability.** Nineteen 2010 WISE profile fits span two visits, 14 frames in January and five in August. Their median W3 magnitudes are 8.632 and 8.760, and median W4 magnitudes are 7.096 and 7.224. Individual W4 S/N is 2.1–5.8. The repeated profile fits disfavor a single bad exposure and establish a repeatable cryogenic-era measurement. Both visits use the same instrument and rely on the same profile-fitting assumptions. [WISE All-Sky single-exposure products](https://wise2.ipac.caltech.edu/docs/release/allsky/expsup/sec2_2a.html)
- **Image localization.** Two separate reviewers fit Gaussian profiles with local tilted backgrounds directly to AllWISE atlas pixels. In the Round 3 fit, nominal W3 and W4 centroid offsets from Gaia propagated to J2010.3346 are 0.34″ and 0.44″. W3 offsets at different fit radii span 0.15–0.36″, and the W4 blank-patch centroid error is about 0.6″ per axis. In independent L1b image stacks, W3 centroids are within 0.62″ of the star in each of the two visits. W3−W2 positions change sign between visits, so the small displacement is sensitive to scan or fit systematics. The August W4 fit has several arcseconds of uncertainty. The resolved PS1 counterpart is point-like and its epoch-matched position is within 0.02″. The available optical images do not establish a completeness limit for crowded or very red objects close to the target. [IRSA W3 atlas FITS](https://nasa-irsa-wise.s3.us-west-2.amazonaws.com/wise/allwise/images/p3am_cdd/04/0440/0440p060_ac51/0440p060_ac51-w3-int-3.fits) · [IRSA W4 atlas FITS](https://nasa-irsa-wise.s3.us-west-2.amazonaws.com/wise/allwise/images/p3am_cdd/04/0440/0440p060_ac51/0440p060_ac51-w4-int-3.fits) · [Atlas resolution](https://irsa.ipac.caltech.edu/data/WISE/docs/release/AllWISE/expsup/sec4_4.html)
- **The 2017–2021 infrared event.** In the 18″ IRSA cone, 283 detections fall within 2″ of the Gaia track. Of those, 277 meet `cc_flags=0000`, `qual_frame>0`, and S/N ≥ 5 in both bands. The 21 visit medians reproduce the brightening and later fading. From the median of the first six visits to peak, W1 brightens by 0.221 mag and W2 by 0.369 mag, giving **~40% more W2 flux**. Peak-to-faintest W2 is 0.411 mag (~46%). Splitting alternating visits gives median bright-phase W2 shifts of 0.366 and 0.263 mag for the two branches. The same-epoch comparison star J025409.21+055101.5, 121″ away with W2 ≈ 9.81, has a 0.045-mag full range and no comparable brightening. The target’s median catalog centroid shifts by 0.026″ between baseline and its W2 peak after motion correction, a descriptive result from the multiband position. A close blend below the resolution and a source-specific PSF bias remain viable. [Target visit medians](evidence/2026-11-17/5628404318169472-neowise-visits.csv) · [Field comparison](evidence/2026-11-17/J025409.21+055101.5-neowise-control.csv)
- **Independent optical coverage.** The successful IRSA ZTF query yielded 1,325 g/r rows from four object IDs at a median epoch-corrected offset of 0.14″, with 1,196 rows at `catflags=0`. In the two main object IDs, yearly medians across 2018–2024 span 0.060 mag in g and 0.055 mag in r. This excludes a sustained ~0.4-mag optical change at the sampled seasons. It allows smaller changes, and the seasonal trend needs local differential calibration to assign an astrophysical amplitude. ZTF starts after the IR rise began. ASAS-SN independently shows a 0.065-mag range in July–June g-band seasons after subtracting same-image residuals from six comparison stars (1,772 coadded epochs, 870 nights). V-band observations cover the rise and show changes of a few hundredths of a magnitude. The data support low-amplitude optical changes during the broader event. [ZTF annual photometry](evidence/2026-11-17/5628404318169472-ztf-annual.csv) · [Differential ASAS-SN photometry](evidence/2026-11-17/asassn-seasonal.csv) · [ASAS-SN Sky Patrol](https://asas-sn.github.io/skypatrol/)
- **TESS cross-extraction.** TIC 387517877 has QLP data in sectors 4, 31, 42–44, 70 and 71. Sector-normalized QLP detrended central 90% ranges are 0.83–1.94% in flux. A separate extraction from TESScut pixels in sectors 4, 31 and 44, with a 1.6-pixel aperture and local sky annulus, yields central 90% ranges of 4.99%, 2.53% and 3.97% at matched cadences. Daily relative fluxes correlate with QLP raw aperture photometry at r = 0.81, 0.43 and 0.45, and agreement with detrended QLP is weaker. These establish coverage and a first extraction comparison. Pixel calibration and detrending differ, and the sector normalization removes inter-sector offsets. [MAST QLP](https://archive.stsci.edu/hlsp/qlp) · [TESScut](https://mast.stsci.edu/tesscut/)

### Findings that change the draft

1. **The single 586 K, 4.4%, 0.096 AU fit does not explain the WISE SED.** After photosphere subtraction, W3/W4 excess in Fν is 0.90. A 586 K blackbody gives 1.93 at the nominal band wavelengths and predicts total W4 ≈ 5.00 mJy when scaled to the measured W3, roughly half the observed flux. A two-band fit gives **305 K** (289–326 K statistical interval) and a conditional luminosity ratio near 1.7%. That component would supply only 0.28 mJy of the ~3.81 mJy W2 residual. Report the fluxes as the reproducible result. Broad response integration, possible emission features, additional temperatures and source confusion need to be addressed before inferring a luminosity, radius or dust-production history. [WISE W4 response correction](https://arxiv.org/abs/1411.5444)
2. **The two infrared measurements have different wavelength and time coverage.** W3/W4 were measured in 2010. NEOWISE measured W1/W2 during the subsequent brightening. The two signatures are compatible with a variable dusty system, including transient debris. They do not establish that 12–22 µm emission varied or that the later event came from the same physical component. Published extreme debris systems demonstrate that multiyear W1/W2 brightening and fading can occur naturally. [NEOWISE data model](https://wise2.ipac.caltech.edu/docs/release/neowise/expsup/sec2_2.html) · [TYC 4209-1322-1 monitoring](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.11856)
3. **The binary and youth exclusions are too strong.** The quoted 2.13 km s⁻¹ is the uncertainty on Gaia's combined RV, and Gaia gives no epoch RV scatter or robust amplitude for this source. RUWE is 1.078 and Gaia reports no non-single-star solution. An unresolved companion, age and the origin of any dust are open. Gaia FLAME reports 0.223 solar luminosities, compared with the draft's 0.182. The difference calls for naming the estimator and propagating the uncertainty in any physical radius. [Gaia source data model](https://gea.esac.esa.int/archive/documentation/GDR3/Gaia_archive/chap_datamodel/sec_dm_main_source_catalogue/ssec_dm_gaia_source.html)
4. **The optical and infrared variability were already catalogued.** AAVSO VSX lists the star as ROT. Its G-band range agrees with Gaia DR3, which gives a low SOLAR_LIKE score of 0.185 and no released rotation-modulation solution. Both VarWISE catalogs contain cluster `2540580983592406` within 0.402″, with matching cross-identifications and photometry. Their W1/W2 amplitude metrics are 0.180/0.279 mag. VarWISE assigns an automated LPV score of 0.7754 and flags its 387/344-day periods as suspect. This is prior recognition of the infrared variability from the same NEOWISE archive. The automated class does not establish an evolved star or explain the origin of the 2010 excess. [AAVSO VSX entry](https://www.aavso.org/vsx/index.php?view=detail.top&oid=2850403) · [VarWISE Pure cone query](https://irsa.ipac.caltech.edu/cgi-bin/Gator/nph-query?catalog=varwisepure&spatial=cone&objstr=43.548272861%205.882712239&radius=20&radunits=arcsec&outfmt=1) · [Paz et al. 2026](https://www.ipac.caltech.edu/publication/2026ApJS..284...41P)
5. **The FAP and independence language overstates the result.** The generic outlier score reuses WISE excess and variability features. It provides no independent confirmation of either measurement. The repeated `1e-12` values and the two search-wide percentages should be accompanied by calibration range, effective trials, the exact counted event and the tested injection population. A significance quoted below the directly resolved empirical tail needs a stated tail model and uncertainty.

## 1111149103659931264 / G 251-44 / LHS 236

- **Independent spectroscopy explains the lead.** SIMBAD links the DR3 identifier to LHS 236, the DR2 identifier 1111149103659451520, and 2MASS J07432434+7248500. Gizis classified it sdK7 from spectra, with published B−V = 1.33. Woolf and Wallerstein measured [Fe/H] = −1.32 ± 0.05, Teff = 4040 ± 20 K, and log g = 4.92 ± 0.16. Gaia GSP-Phot independently gives [M/H] = −1.088 and 4100 K. The Gaia photometric global metallicity and the spectroscopic iron abundance are distinct measurements. BP−RP = 1.664 and Mg = 8.680 before extinction correction (8.468 using Gaia's reported extinction). The optical color is consistent with the established class. The pipeline's z-score needs a temperature- and metallicity-conditioned reference population before it can carry physical meaning. [SIMBAD](https://simbad.cfa.harvard.edu/simbad/sim-id?Ident=Gaia+DR3+1111149103659931264) · [Gizis 1997, Tables 2 and 4](https://adsabs.harvard.edu/pdf/1997AJ....113..806G) · [Woolf and Wallerstein 2005, Table 3](https://arxiv.org/abs/astro-ph/0410452)
- **High proper motion biases unpropagated comparisons.** Parallax is 17.00662 ± 0.01365 mas (58.80 pc) and proper motion is 1.2237″ yr⁻¹, giving 341.10 km s⁻¹ tangentially. The J2016 Gaia position is 6.66″ from AllWISE. Propagating to the actual catalog mean epoch reduces the separation to 0.057″. Several PS1 object IDs land on the motion track at their own mean epochs. Their inconsistent PSF/Kron magnitudes and split detections make a single merged PS1 SED unreliable. Gaia astrometric quality is good (RUWE 1.018 and `ipd_frac_multi_peak=0`). Gaia RV is +37.53 ± 0.76 km s⁻¹ with 28 transits and a constant-RV p-value of 0.77. The older +66.8 km s⁻¹ measurement differs by about 1.5 times the study’s typical velocity error of 20 km s⁻¹. [Gaia astrometry and RV fields](https://gea.esac.esa.int/archive/documentation/GDR3/Gaia_archive/chap_datamodel/sec_dm_main_source_catalogue/ssec_dm_gaia_source.html)
- **No significant mid-IR signal.** W3 = 9.657 ± 0.045 mag sits at or below a simple photospheric extrapolation. W4 = 8.377 mag is an upper limit (`AAAU`, `w4nm=0`). Treat W4 as censored in any excess analysis. The motion-matched NEOWISE series has 399 usable detections in **21 visits**. Visit medians span 0.015 mag in W1 and 0.020 mag in W2. The log's nine visits are an incomplete baseline. [Epoch-matched visit medians](evidence/2026-11-17/1111149103659931264-neowise-visits.csv)
- **Optical photometry is sensitive to the moving scene.** TIC 441602956 matches the DR2 identity and has usable QLP products in seven sectors. Detrended central 90% flux ranges are 0.25–0.72% after independent sector normalization. The motion-centered TESScut extractions in sectors 20 and 60 give matched-cadence ranges of 0.48% and 1.57%. Daily flux correlations with raw QLP are 0.21 and −0.31. Full agreement remains open. The fixed-coordinate ASAS-SN g medians drift by about 0.31 mag over seven years while the star moves 8.6″, comparable to the survey's pixel scale. The drift is vulnerable to aperture losses and needs motion-fitting before use. The ZTF cone requests timed out. [MAST QLP](https://archive.stsci.edu/hlsp/qlp) · [ASAS-SN photometry](evidence/2026-11-17/asassn-seasonal.csv)

## Reproduction and review scope

[Archive query manifest](evidence/2026-11-17/archive-queries.json) records exact request URLs, SHA-256 hashes of responses and query outcomes for the coordinator's archive analysis. Raw responses are excluded from the repository. Camera/scan repeats within a single mission test repeatability. Measurements from separate instruments and published spectroscopy test independent physical claims. The flux check uses $F_\nu=F_{0,\nu}10^{-0.4m}$ and zero points of 666.7, 309.54, 171.787, 31.674 and 8.363 Jy for Ks/W1–W4. The Planck photosphere is normalized at Ks and evaluated at 2.159, 3.353, 4.603, 11.561 and 22.088 µm. Subtract the photospheric flux and solve for the Planck temperature that gives the observed W3/W4 excess-flux ratio. Statistical bounds use 8,000 Gaussian draws from Ks/W3/W4 magnitude errors propagated into flux, with stellar temperature fixed. The NEOWISE analysis sorts by MJD, assigns a new visit for gaps ≥30 days and takes medians. The CSV's MAD columns show within-visit scatter. For ASAS-SN use the public `SkyPatrolClient.cone_search` at both J2016 positions with a 15″ radius, `catalog='stellar_main'` and `download=True`. Select `quality='G'` and 0 < mag_err < 0.05. For the infrared lead, subtract each star's per-camera median, then subtract the median residual of at least three controls on each matching image. The six control IDs and endpoint are recorded in the Round 2 record. The seasonal intervals bootstrap 1,500 nightly-median draws with seed 12 and omit common systematic error.

Three reviewers were started in separate contexts. Round 1 received an unlabeled list of four live Gaia sources, including two same-field comparison positions. The list used live astrometry because the standard packet builder falls back to fixture positions for live IDs outside the fixture catalog. Those controls exercise counterpart ambiguity and do not constitute a blind validation against a known-positive sample. Rounds 2 and 3 received only candidate identifiers, positions and the numerical claims under test. Each retrieved public observations independently. A second model family was unavailable in the configured environment. The human checklist captures the remaining work and still requires sign-off.
